How it works

The page reads SPHEREx Level-2 images directly from NASA's public archive bucket. A static index lists every exposure and where it points. For each exposure that covers your position the page downloads the FITS header and only the detector rows it needs, resamples them north-up, and subtracts the local sky level. Nothing runs on a server.

Each exposure sees a different wavelength, because SPHEREx uses a filter whose colour changes across the detector. Adding the exposures of one detector gives a deep image in one of six broad bands.

Planet X stack

A body orbiting the Sun at a given distance drifts across the sky in a known way: the Earth's motion makes it wobble (parallax) and its own orbit makes it creep. The page removes the fixed stars, shifts every exposure of one visit along that track and adds them. A real body at that distance sharpens into a point; everything else smears out. Beyond about 300 AU a body barely moves within one visit, so its distance comes from finding it again in another epoch.
